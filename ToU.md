# 2. What BSA Is

BSA is an open-source mapping and environmental awareness application.

It attempts to answer questions like:

- "What's around me?"
- "Where's the nearest fuel station?"
- "What's that camera?"
- "Can I get there without making three unnecessary left turns?"

It does not attempt to:

- Read your mind.
- Predict the future.
- Replace common sense.
- Win arguments with your spouse.
- Convince your GPS satellite to work indoors.

---

# 3. Maps Are Imperfect

OpenStreetMap is built by an incredible community.

It is also maintained by humans.

Humans occasionally:

- Make mistakes.
- Move things.
- Forget things.
- Accidentally place a surveillance camera on the wrong side of the road.

If you discover inaccurate mapping, please consider improving OpenStreetMap for everyone's benefit.

# 4. Permissions

Applications often ask for permissions.

Sometimes those permissions make sense.

Sometimes they absolutely do not.

Let's look at a few examples.

## Contacts

Some applications request access to your contacts.

This allows them to:

- Find friends.
- Build social graphs.
- Recommend people.
- Upload your address book.

BSA does none of those things.

Knowing who your dentist is does not improve navigation.

Therefore:

> BSA does not request access to your contacts.

## Calendar

Some applications want your calendar.

Unless your calendar contains a secret shortcut around traffic...

...BSA doesn't need it.

## Camera

Maps do not require cameras.

Future optional infrastructure documentation features may.

If that day comes, camera access will remain optional.

## Microphone

Voice search is a perfectly legitimate navigation feature.

BSA currently does not implement one.

If microphone access is ever requested, it will be for a feature you intentionally choose to use.

---

# 5. Why This Matters

Every permission grants software access to additional information.

Sometimes that's necessary.

Sometimes it's merely convenient.

Sometimes it's difficult to justify.

Good software should request only the permissions required for its functionality.

Understanding why an application requests a permission is often more important than simply accepting it.

We encourage users to ask questions whenever software requests access that seems unrelated to its purpose.

---

# 6. Online Services

BSA tries to keep as much processing local as practical.

However...

Maps don't magically appear.

Routing engines don't spontaneously calculate routes.

Destination searches don't happen telepathically.

Some features necessarily rely on external services such as:

- OpenStreetMap
- Overpass
- Nominatim
- OSRM

Those services receive the information necessary to answer your request.

BSA itself is not operating those services.

---

# 7. Surveillance Data

BSA displays information contributed to OpenStreetMap.

Displayed surveillance infrastructure may be:

- Accurate
- Inaccurate
- Outdated
- Missing entirely

Use your own judgment.

Seeing a camera marker does not necessarily mean that:

- it records,
- it identifies,
- it works,
- or it still exists.

Likewise...

Not seeing one doesn't prove the opposite.
